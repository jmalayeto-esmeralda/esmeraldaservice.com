Importing Goods and Customs Clearance in Venezuela

We review the operation from purchase order through cargo collection. The advice focuses on which goods can enter, the applicable customs procedure, the supporting documents and the issues that could affect landed cost or delivery.

Discuss an import matterExplore services
The legal decision

Start with product specifications before booking freight

A commercial label such as “industrial equipment” rarely provides enough detail for import review. Function, materials, condition and how an item is presented can affect tariff classification and product controls.

We review country of origin and the evidence supporting it alongside the invoice, terms of sale, freight and insurance. These details inform customs valuation and the estimated cost of importing the goods.

How we can help

  • Pre-shipment review
  • Tariff, value and origin
  • Clearance and cargo release
  • Post-clearance follow-up

Pre-shipment review

We identify the importer, intended use, customs procedure and permits to have in place before dispatch.

Tariff, value and origin

We review the product details with the authorized agency and check the proposed tariff treatment against the commercial records. We estimate taxes and duties separately from logistics charges.

Clearance and cargo release

We coordinate pre-filing document review and follow up on customs examination, assessment, payment and cargo collection as applicable.

Post-clearance follow-up

We review record retention, unresolved discrepancies and product-specific labeling or market requirements that may apply after release.

What to prepare for the initial review

  • Specifications and description by model or reference
  • Commercial or pro forma invoice, quantities and terms of sale
  • Origin, place of dispatch, freight, insurance and planned route
  • Any permits already obtained and current shipment status

Frequently asked questions

Can you review a purchase before we book freight?

Yes. This gives the importer time to confirm technical information with the supplier, check permits and assess the landed cost.

What does nationalización mean for an import?

For a definitive import, it refers to completing the applicable entry formalities, including the declaration, controls and any duties or taxes due, so customs can release the goods.
